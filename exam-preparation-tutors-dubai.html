A useful starting point

Six details that shape an effective exam plan

Exam preparation works best when the tutor knows the exact course, current position and time available.

01

Curriculum and board

Share GCSE, IGCSE, A Level or IB, plus the examination board and specification where known.

02

Subject and paper

Identify the subject, component or paper rather than asking for general revision alone.

03

Current evidence

Recent mock results, marked work and teacher feedback help show where marks are being lost.

04

Target and timeline

Include the target grade and dates for mocks, internal assessments or final examinations.

05

Priority gaps

Note weak topics, unfinished content, timing problems or question types that cause difficulty.

06

Lesson format

Online tuition can widen specialist choice; in-home availability depends on location and timetable.

Beyond more revision

Triage the cause before choosing the practice

A disappointing mock result can come from different causes: missing knowledge, weak recall, inaccurate method, poor interpretation, incomplete written responses or time pressure. Those problems need different kinds of support.

A tutor can review available evidence, establish priorities and use board-relevant questions to test whether an improvement is secure. The aim is a focused plan, not simply a larger volume of worksheets.

Knowledge

Re-teach essential ideas and connect them to the wording used in the course.

Application

Practise selecting and applying knowledge in unfamiliar or multi-step questions.

Written responses

Improve structure, precision and use of evidence where extended answers are assessed.

Timing

Use timed sections selectively, then analyse errors before repeating the task.

Questions from parents

Frequently asked questions

Which qualifications can exam preparation tuition support?

Families can request support for GCSE, IGCSE, A Level and International Baccalaureate courses. Include the exact board, subject, level and paper so relevant tutor experience can be considered.

Can a tutor help before mock exams?

Yes. Share the mock dates and recent work so the tutor can help prioritise topics, paper skills and a realistic revision sequence.

Will lessons use past papers?

Past papers and mark schemes can be used appropriately once the course is confirmed. The most useful approach combines targeted questions with careful review of why marks were lost.

How early should exam preparation begin?

That depends on the current level, target and size of the gaps. An earlier enquiry gives more time to diagnose needs, teach missing content and check progress before timed practice.

Is online exam preparation available?

Yes. Families can request online, in-home or flexible tuition. In-person availability depends on location and timetable.
